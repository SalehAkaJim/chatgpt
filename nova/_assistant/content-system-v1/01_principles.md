# 01 — Core principles

## 1. Learner reality

The learner is a Persian speaker with zero assumed English knowledge.

- Never require an English word, grammar pattern, pronunciation contrast, spelling convention, or task mechanic before it has been made learnable.
- Early English must be short, high-frequency, useful, and immediately meaningful.
- Difficulty increases through controlled complexity, not through abrupt vocabulary or sentence-length jumps.
- Persian support is a scaffold, not a permanent crutch; it decreases gradually as comprehension grows.

## 2. Communication before coverage

Every Chapter must answer one question: **what can the learner do after this that they could not reliably do before?**

A Chapter is justified by a communicative outcome, not by a topic list or a quota.

Examples of acceptable outcomes:
- greet someone and respond naturally;
- say and ask a name;
- say where you are from and understand the same question;
- request a simple item politely.

A Chapter whose only goal is “teach these 12 words” is invalid.

## 3. Meaning → noticing → retrieval → transfer

New language normally moves through these states:

1. understandable exposure in context;
2. attention to meaning/form/sound when needed;
3. supported response;
4. retrieval with less support;
5. use in a changed context.

These are learning states, not mandatory fixed Lessons. One Lesson may cover several states; difficult material may need more than one Lesson.

## 4. Natural language only

Story/dialogue is used only when it is a natural vehicle for the outcome.

- Never distort dialogue to repeat a target phrase unnaturally.
- Repetition belongs in learning interactions, not fake human conversation.
- A micro-dialogue, short message, sign, menu, form, instruction, or monologue may be better than a story.
- Target English must be something a real speaker could naturally say in that situation.

## 5. No hidden prerequisites

A scored task may contain only:
- already taught target units;
- transparent support language whose meaning is available in the task;
- deliberately introduced new language when the task is exposure-only and not scored.

If success requires guessing an untaught grammar pattern or vocabulary item, the task fails QA.

## 6. Flexible lesson design

There is no required activity checklist.

Listening, speaking, reading, writing, pronunciation, grammar explanation, vocabulary teaching, recognition, recall, and transfer are included only when they serve the current outcome.

A short Lesson with strong evidence is better than a padded Lesson. A longer Lesson is acceptable when the outcome genuinely needs it.

## 7. English text quality is a blocking gate

For the English pilot, every learner-visible English line must be auditable by a fluent human before scale-up.

English must be:
- grammatical;
- idiomatic for the intended context;
- consistent in register;
- appropriate for the learner level;
- internally consistent in spelling and punctuation;
- free from generator-like repetition and awkward literal phrasing.

## 8. Persian is learner-facing teaching language

Persian translations/explanations must communicate meaning naturally.

- Do not force word-for-word Persian when it sounds wrong.
- Use literal alignment only when a learning task explicitly needs it.
- Prefer clear everyday Persian over academic terminology.
- Grammar metalanguage is optional and minimized at beginner level.

## 9. Mastery is performance, not file presence

A Chapter is not complete because required files exist or row counts match.

Completion requires evidence that the learner has had a fair chance to understand, retrieve, and use the target outcome.

## 10. One source of truth

Human/AI authoring happens in one canonical Chapter source. SQL, QA summaries, manifests, and audio jobs are derived outputs.

No generated derivative may become a competing content source.
