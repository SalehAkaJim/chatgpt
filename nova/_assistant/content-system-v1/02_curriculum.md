# 02 — Curriculum rules

## CEFR usage

CEFR is an outcome framework, not a chapter-number template.

Nova maps content to learner actions such as reception, interaction, production and simple mediation where relevant. The Level ends when required outcomes have sufficient learning and assessment evidence; it does not end at a predefined Chapter number.

## Scope control

Only A1 is designed and produced initially.

A2/B1/B2 content must not be generated merely to fill a roadmap. Later levels begin only after the A1 system and pilot demonstrate acceptable quality.

## Outcome model

Each curriculum outcome contains:
- stable outcome ID;
- learner-facing can-do statement in Persian;
- CEFR mode(s): reception / interaction / production / mediation;
- real-world context(s);
- prerequisite outcomes;
- core lexical units;
- core constructions/grammar;
- pronunciation needs;
- evidence requirements;
- status: planned / introduced / practiced / mastered.

## Chapter planning

A Chapter normally owns one primary communicative outcome and may reinforce earlier outcomes.

A Chapter may have one or more Lessons. The planner decides this from instructional load and transfer needs, not a fixed template.

Reject a Chapter plan when:
- it bundles unrelated outcomes only to increase volume;
- it exists solely to teach a vocabulary list;
- it introduces too many independent grammar ideas at once;
- it depends on language not yet available;
- its real-world purpose cannot be explained clearly.

## A1 progression

Progression is controlled across these dimensions:

1. **communicative demand** — response formula → controlled choice → short independent response;
2. **sentence complexity** — chunks/simple clauses before combinations;
3. **lexical load** — high-frequency concrete language before less frequent/abstract language;
4. **listening load** — slow clear isolated exchange before longer connected speech;
5. **reading load** — short labels/messages before short connected text;
6. **pronunciation load** — intelligibility-critical contrasts introduced gradually;
7. **support level** — Persian/visible model/high support gradually reduced;
8. **context variation** — familiar context before transfer to a changed context.

No single numeric difficulty score is allowed to substitute for these dimensions.

## New-language budget

New language is capped, not quota-filled.

For absolute-zero and early A1 Lessons, prefer a small set of new lexical/construction units that can actually be practiced. A Lesson may legitimately introduce only one or two important items.

Do not add new words merely to reach a minimum count.

## Review system

Review is obligation-based rather than hardcoded to exact Chapter offsets.

Each important learning unit has a recurrence priority and review windows. The planner must create meaningful later retrieval, but natural reuse in new outcomes is preferred over artificial review drills.

Review evidence must require recognition, recall, comprehension or production. Mere appearance in a dialogue does not count as successful review.

## Level completion

A1 can be marked complete only when:
- every required A1 outcome is mastered or explicitly excluded with rationale;
- core high-frequency language has recurring evidence across contexts;
- reception and interaction are not represented only by scripted repetition;
- pronunciation/intelligibility targets have evidence;
- reading and basic written-language handling have evidence appropriate to product capabilities;
- cumulative transfer checks show recombination of earlier language.

Chapter count is whatever this requires.
