# A1 communicative outcome map — English → Persian

This is a coverage map, not a Chapter list. One outcome may need more than one Lesson; closely related outcomes may share a Chapter when instructional load remains small and coherent.

The sequence is refined through prerequisites and pilot evidence.

## Stage 0 — first successful interaction

- `A1-INT-001` — greet someone and respond to a greeting.
- `A1-INT-002` — say your name and ask someone their name.
- `A1-INT-003` — use and understand essential social formulas: yes/no, please, thank you, sorry, excuse me.
- `A1-REC-001` — understand very short, carefully delivered questions/instructions when context is clear.
- `A1-STRAT-001` — signal that you do not understand and ask for repetition in a simple learned form.

## Stage 1 — basic personal information

- `A1-INT-004` — say where you are from and ask where another person is from.
- `A1-INT-005` — give/understand simple age and number information where relevant.
- `A1-INT-006` — give very basic contact/personal details such as city or phone-number style information.
- `A1-PROD-001` — give a few simple connected facts about yourself using learned patterns.
- `A1-WRITE-001` — complete a very simple personal-information form.

## Stage 2 — people and things around you

- `A1-INT-007` — identify a person or common object and ask what/who it is.
- `A1-PROD-002` — describe a familiar person/object with a few basic properties.
- `A1-INT-008` — say what you have/do not have and ask the same in a simple context.
- `A1-REC-002` — understand basic descriptions when supported by context/image.

## Stage 3 — needs, requests and simple transactions

- `A1-INT-009` — ask for a common item politely and respond to a simple request.
- `A1-INT-010` — say what you want/need in a familiar situation.
- `A1-INT-011` — ask and understand a simple price.
- `A1-INT-012` — handle very simple quantity/number exchanges.
- `A1-INT-013` — order a basic food/drink item and respond to the immediate follow-up.

## Stage 4 — place and movement

- `A1-INT-014` — ask where a familiar place/object is.
- `A1-REC-003` — understand short simple location/direction instructions with contextual support.
- `A1-INT-015` — ask for a simple direction and confirm basic understanding.
- `A1-PROD-003` — state where a familiar person/object/place is using basic location language.

## Stage 5 — time and everyday routine

- `A1-INT-016` — ask/tell simple clock time when needed in a familiar situation.
- `A1-INT-017` — understand/use basic days/dates for simple arrangements.
- `A1-PROD-004` — describe a very simple daily routine with short learned constructions.
- `A1-INT-018` — ask/answer simple questions about routine or frequency.

## Stage 6 — preferences, ability and simple plans

- `A1-INT-019` — say and ask simple likes/dislikes.
- `A1-INT-020` — say what you can/cannot do and ask about simple ability.
- `A1-INT-021` — make/respond to a very simple invitation or suggestion.
- `A1-INT-022` — make a basic arrangement using familiar time/place language.

## Stage 7 — home, family and familiar life

- `A1-PROD-005` — give a few basic facts about close family/people in your life.
- `A1-PROD-006` — give a basic description of home or a familiar place.
- `A1-INT-023` — ask/answer simple questions about familiar people, home and everyday possessions.

## Stage 8 — simple problems and help

- `A1-INT-024` — say that there is a simple problem or that you need help.
- `A1-INT-025` — handle a very short clarification exchange: repeat, slower, which one, where, etc.
- `A1-REC-004` — follow common short public/service instructions relevant to beginner situations.

## Reading outcomes

- `A1-READ-001` — understand common signs, labels and interface-style words in a clear context.
- `A1-READ-002` — understand the key meaning of a very short personal/service message.
- `A1-READ-003` — extract names, numbers, times, prices and locations from simple text.

## Written interaction/production outcomes

- `A1-WRITE-002` — write/assemble a very short message using learned language: greeting + one simple fact/request + closing where appropriate.
- `A1-WRITE-003` — provide short personal answers in written form with strong support.

## Simple mediation outcome

- `A1-MED-001` — relay a very simple piece of concrete information such as a name, number, time, place or basic request when the context is clear.

## Enabling language inventory

Likely A1 constructions include, when required by outcomes:
- `be` for identity/basic description;
- subject pronouns and basic possessives;
- `this/that`, basic singular/plural reference;
- simple `what/where/who/how` questions;
- basic yes/no questions;
- `have`, `want`, `need`, `like`, `can`;
- present simple for familiar routine;
- imperatives used in common instructions;
- basic articles/determiners as needed for natural English;
- common prepositions of place/time;
- `there is/are` where it supports location/description;
- high-value chunks such as greetings, polite requests and clarification formulas.

This is not a grammar-teaching order. Constructions enter only when an outcome needs them and prerequisites allow them.

## A1 pronunciation/intelligibility track for Persian speakers

Targets are introduced only in words/contexts where they matter. Likely priorities include:
- English word stress and unstressed vowel awareness;
- `w` vs Persian-influenced `v` substitution;
- `th` sounds when high-frequency words require them;
- final consonants and plural/third-person endings where meaning depends on them;
- basic consonant clusters without inserting extra vowels;
- selected high-impact vowel contrasts such as `ship/sheep` type distinctions when lexically relevant;
- natural contractions/reductions after the full form is understood.

The goal is intelligibility, not removal of a Persian accent.
