# German Pre-A1 Curriculum Blueprint

Status: planning
Lesson count status: provisional
Provisional lesson count: 30
Target database: MySQL 9.0.1

## Principle

The lesson count is not fixed in advance. It is derived from CEFR Pre-A1 coverage, German-specific needs, source availability, repetition needs and QA. The current number (30) is provisional and can move up or down after every lesson has an exact source mapping.

No German teaching sentence, dialogue, example or grammar explanation may be invented by the app-content process. Target-language teaching content must come from a traceable source. Persian is the translation/support layer.

Every lesson must start with a source-backed conversation_speaking activity. The number and order of all later activities are dynamic.

## Primary source stack

1. Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume (2020): curriculum/level reference only; not used as lesson text.
2. FSI German Programmed Introduction: primary public-domain source for beginner dialogues, pronunciation work, drills and formulaic language.
3. German Headstart (DLI/FSI archive): primary public-domain source for survival situations such as greetings, numbers, directions, food, money, time, dates, shopping and telephone use.
4. FSI German Basic Course: public-domain backup source for dialogues, vocabulary and structural notes when the two sources above do not provide clean enough coverage.
5. Tatoeba: supplemental sentence source only when a gap remains; every reused sentence must retain contributor/license attribution. Text is generally CC BY 2.0 FR, with some CC0 material.

## CEFR Pre-A1 interpretation used here

Pre-A1 is treated as a milestone below A1: the learner mainly relies on isolated words and memorised/formulaic expressions rather than freely generating language. Coverage therefore prioritises recognition, rehearsed speaking, very basic information exchange and highly predictable everyday situations.

## Coverage clusters

The course must cover all of these before Pre-A1 can be marked final:

- recognise and use basic greetings and leave-taking
- recognise/use basic courtesy formulas such as yes/no/please/thank-you/sorry/excuse-me
- understand very short, slowly delivered questions and statements with contextual support
- understand basic personal information when spoken slowly and clearly
- give and ask for a name
- give/understand age
- give/understand country/origin and simple residence information
- recognise immediate family labels and give minimal family information
- understand/use simple numbers in everyday exchanges
- give/understand a phone number
- ask/give day, time of day and date
- ask/give date of birth
- understand familiar place words and very simple location information
- ask a very simple information question and understand a one- or two-word answer
- understand very short action instructions in familiar contexts
- understand familiar words/names/numbers in very slow recordings
- recognise familiar words with pictures
- recognise time/place information in very simple notes
- understand event/day/time/location in a very simple invitation
- understand the simplest image-supported informational material
- understand extremely short everyday signs/instructions with visual support
- make a very simple purchase or order food/drink with gesture/pointing support
- recognise prices and simple transactional numbers
- describe oneself with rehearsed/formulaic words (name, age, family)
- state a simple feeling using a small fixed repertoire
- write basic personal information such as name/address/nationality
- write short phrases containing basic personal information
- fill a very simple registration form
- use very simple word order in short statements
- build intelligible pronunciation of the limited rehearsed repertoire, with explicit German sound work distributed through lessons

## Why 30 for now

The count is based on the distinct CEFR Pre-A1 functional clusters above plus German-specific pronunciation onboarding and deliberate consolidation. Several clusters need separate receptive and spoken practice, but closely related targets are merged where the same source dialogue can teach them naturally.

30 is therefore provisional, not a quota. A lesson may be removed when two targets are cleanly covered by one source conversation, or added when source material shows that one target needs more staged exposure.

## Exit rule

German Pre-A1 becomes final only when:

- every mandatory curriculum target is linked to at least one lesson;
- every lesson has an exact source-backed opening conversation;
- target-language strings and grammar notes have provenance;
- the activity sequence has passed repetition QA;
- speaker/character gender, age, role and context have passed QA;
- there are no unresolved CEFR coverage gaps;
- the lesson count has been changed from provisional to final.

Audio remains blocked until the complete German course (all CEFR levels) is final.
